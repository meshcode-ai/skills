# Marketing Psychology — Ethical Application of Persuasion Principles

**Psychological principles are an accelerator, not a brake.** Layering psychology onto a bad product raises short-term conversion and raises churn with it. Before applying, pass the check: "does this principle make the truth look faster?"

## Core Principles and Application Judgment

| Principle | Mechanism | Application | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scarcity | Loss aversion | State stock/deadline | Fake countdowns → brand extinction |
| Social proof | Uncertainty reduction | Numbers + concrete context ("3,200 teams") | Fake reviews, contextless star lists |
| Authority | Reliance on expertise | Credentials, cited research | Irrelevant celebrity |
| Reciprocity | Debt psychology | Give something free first (guide) | Selling the moment it's received |
| Anchoring | Reliance on a reference point | Show list price alongside, expose highest first | Fake list price |
| Indifference | Decision avoidance | Nudge default (premium) selection | Dark patterns = legal risk |
| Loss aversion | Losses > gains | "What you lose" framing | Fear-mongering |

## Application Checklist (pre-check)

1. Is it true? — Is the scarcity genuinely limited?
2. Does the context fit? — B2B buyers lean authority; consumers lean social proof
3. No regret? — If 24 hours after clicking they feel "I got played", cut it
4. Legal? — Deceptive scarcity and fake reviews risk consumer-law and advertising-law violations
